The stack effect

How Crawl Space Moisture Threatens Spokane Homes

Up to 50% of the air on your main floor rises directly from your crawl space. When dirt crawl spaces absorb spring snowmelt and river basin moisture, subterranean humidity skyrockets above 70%.

Unsealed crawl spaces invite wood-decaying fungi (white rot, brown cubical rot, and black mold), warping hardwood floors, sagging subfloor joists, and creating stubborn earthy odors throughout living rooms. Our comprehensive remediation and encapsulation stop ground moisture at the source, preventing costly structural repairs and purifying your indoor breathing air.

How does crawl space mold affect the air inside my Spokane home?

Due to a building science principle known as the 'stack effect,' up to 50% of the air you breathe on your main floor originates in your crawl space. As warm air rises through the home and exits via the attic, it pulls subterranean crawl space air—along with mold spores, dust mites, damp soil vapors, and musty odors—directly upward through floorboards, plumbing chases, and electrical gaps.

How much does crawl space mold remediation and encapsulation cost in Spokane?

In Spokane, crawl space mold remediation typically ranges from $1,800 to $4,500 for standard joist sanitization and new vapor barrier replacement. Complete crawl space encapsulation systems—including antimicrobial joist wash, contaminated insulation removal, 20-mil sealed vapor barrier, and commercial dehumidification—range from $4,500 to $8,500 depending on square footage.

What is the difference between a standard plastic tarp and encapsulation?

Standard 6-mil black plastic often laid loosely on dirt tears easily, holds standing water, and leaves foundation walls exposed. Full encapsulation installs heavy, puncture-resistant 12 to 20-mil reinforced antimicrobial vapor barrier that is permanently taped at seams and mechanically fastened 6-12 inches up foundation walls, completely locking subterranean ground moisture out.

Should crawl space foundation vents be open or closed in Spokane?

In modern building science, closed and encapsulated crawl spaces perform significantly better in Spokane's climate. Open vents let in freezing winter air (which freezes plumbing pipes and chills subfloors) and humid spring/summer air (which condenses on cool crawl space surfaces, causing rot). Conditioning or dehumidifying an encapsulated space prevents condensation year-round.
